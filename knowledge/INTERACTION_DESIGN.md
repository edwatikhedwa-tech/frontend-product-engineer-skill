# Interaction Design

Design the state transition, not just the resting surface. For each important interaction define trigger, feedback, result, error, cancellation, and recovery.

Check hover, focus, active/pressed, selected, disabled, loading, empty, optimistic, success, error, confirmation, permission, and recovery states where relevant. Use motion to clarify continuity and hierarchy; prefer CSS transitions for simple changes and consider Motion only for real gesture, spring, shared-layout, or complex sequencing needs.

Controls should have a clear affordance and a predictable hit area. Destructive actions need an appropriate confirmation or undo strategy. Avoid hidden gestures when a visible control is required for discoverability or accessibility.
